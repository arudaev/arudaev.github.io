CheXVision

two models looked at 112,120 chest x-rays. the borrowed one did better.

a four-person university project on the nih chestx-ray14 set: fourteen pathologies, one picture each.

we trained a residual network from nothing and a densenet-121 borrowed from imagenet, under the same protocol. the borrowed one reached 0.846, a hair above the published chexnet 0.841. the one we built ourselves reached 0.814.

educational research. not a clinical product, and it says so on every page.

Deutsche Bahn Delay Predictor

two million train records. the board still says +12 min.

a three-person university study. the idea was the easiest one in the room: predict how late a deutsche bahn train will be.

the work was not easy. we cleaned 2,061,357 records, chose 16 features by forward selection, and compared linear regression, k-nearest neighbours and a random forest on held-out data. the random forest won, its test error 92.4% below a naive baseline.

the paper is written in quarto and rebuilds itself from the notebook. the train on this island has not arrived yet.

Waste Sorting Assistant

four kinds of german bin. a camera that knows which is which.

international students in deggendorf get a bin system explained only in german. biomüll, glas, papier, restmüll, and fines when you guess wrong.

three of us walked around town and photographed 466 real bins, labelled them by hand, and fine-tuned yolov8 on them. it runs live on a phone camera through a streamlit app.

i did the devops and the deployment. the lesson: start from a pretrained model, but photograph your own bins.

CheXVision Mini

the same x-rays at 64 by 64, and every gradient written by hand.

chexvision's small sibling: a network written in plain numpy, the forward pass and every gradient by hand. no pytorch, no autograd, cpu only, on purpose.

a 64×64 x-ray, flattened to 4,096 numbers, through 1,024, 256 and 64 to one answer. the gradients pass a check against finite differences.

it doesn't reach the big model's numbers, and doesn't pretend to. it reached a test auc of 0.650 after four hours on a kaggle cpu.

Project Apero

assessing personal event risks online. out here, on the rocks.

a course project for assistant systems at thd, winter 2024/25: a chatbot that walks you through a stroke-risk model.

streamlit in front, a rasa assistant to talk to, and three scikit-learn models behind it: logistic regression, a random forest and an svm, trained on the public kaggle stroke set of 5,110 rows, padded with about 30% synthetic ones.

strokes are rare in the data, so the honest number is low: an f1 of 0.21 on the real rows. the name came first, the acronym later.
